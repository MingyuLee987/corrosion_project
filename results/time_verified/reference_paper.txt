A supervised machine learning approach
with dual GAN augmentation and Bayesian
HPO for corrosion prediction of Mg-
Li alloys
Check for updates

The current study overcomes the limitations of Mg-Li alloys by creating a dataset with 450 data points
sourced from literature. Two generative adversarial network (GAN) architectures, non-saturating GAN
(NSGAN) with residual generator blocks, spectral normalization and feature matching loss, and
Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) were used to generate augmented datasets.
WGAN-GP had relatively better attribute ﬁdelity to the KS/correlation values with composite score of
0.641 than NSGAN augmented data with score of 0.629. NSGAN-augmented data provided ML R2
mean of 0.752 than WGAN-GP with mean of 0.716 across all corrosion targets such as Ecorr, icorr, CR
and H2 evolution rate. The NSGAN augmented dataset were combined with experimental data and
Bayesian hyperparameter optimization was performed using Optuna m-TPE across ﬁve ML models,
namely RF, XGBoost, SVR, AdaBoost and CatBoost. The integrated Experimental+NSGAN
+CatBoost-HPO framework obtained test R2 values of 0.867, 0.814, 0.814 and 0.763 for Ecorr, icorr, CR
and H2 evolution rate, respectively, which are 12.5%, 13.7%, 13.8% and 15.3% higher than
experimental CatBoost-HPO baseline. SHAP analysis and permutation importance identiﬁed crystal
structure as the dominant predictor for all targets. This framework establishes NSGAN-based
augmentation as a preferred approach for electrochemical data of Mg-Li alloys.
Mg-Li alloys are the lightest structural metal material with densities of
1.25 g/cm3 to 1.65 g/cm3, being 3/4th of conventional Mg alloys and less
than 2/3rd of aluminium alloys1,2. The combination of ultra-low density,
speciﬁc stiffness, electromagnetic damping, and biocompatibility of Mg-Li
alloys has found its applications in satellite structures, aerospace airframes,
biodegradable orthopaedic implants and next-generation anodes for Li-air
batteries3,4. However, their aqueous corrosion behaviour has restricted the
alloys from wider applications. Mg and Li have negative standard potential
of −2.37 V and −3.04 V, respectively, and the resulting oxides MgO and
Li2O have Pilling–Bedworth ratio below the unity, fail to form passive ﬁlm
under extreme conditions5,6. Similarly, the α-Mg and β-Mg-Li phases show
different electrochemical potentials, where micro-galvanic corrosion occurs
within the phases before the chloride ions interact7. Therefore,the corrosion
behavior and prediction for Mg-Li alloys under different electrolyte still
remains a challenge for researchers.
The corrosion behaviour of Mg-Li alloys is determined by Li content,
phase compositions such as α-hcp, β-bcc and α + β, grain size, stability of
the surface ﬁlm and environmental factors such as chloride ion
concentration8,9. Minor alloying elements such as Al, Zn, Mn, Ca, RE, Sn, Y,
and Zr have the effects of reﬁning grain size, deterioration of second phase
nucleation and initiation of galvanic couples through precipitates such as
MgZn2, AlLi and Mg17Al12
10,11. Thermomechanical processing such as
rolling, extrusion, ECAP and FSP further modiﬁes grain size, dislocation
density, crystallographic texture and residual stress which affects corrosion
kinetics12. Electrolyte parameters such as chloride ion concentration, pH,
temperature and immersion time affect icorr based on the composition of
alloy13. The traditional experimental procedure of casting, processing,
immersion and potentiodynamic scanning to extract Tafel slopes is time-
consuming, expensive and limited by samples. Computational approaches
such as DFT and CALPHAD are important tools in materials informatics,

Supervised machine learning has established a relationship between
experimental data and high-dimensional property prediction in corrosion
science18,19.Decisiontreeensemblemodelling,kernelapproachesandneural
networks have been utilized in the study of corrosion of stainless steel,
aluminium alloys, carbon steels and MPEAs. The predicted R2 values range
from 0.80 to 0.93 for few hundred to thousands of experimental data which
are currently available20,21. Lu et al. trained four regression models, namely
RF, MLR, SVR, XGBoost using limited corrosion potential dataset based on
alloy composition and physical characteristics for Mg alloys22. Moses et al.
predicted the electrochemical corrosion behaviour of Mg alloys with seven
ML models and demonstrated XGBoost with high performance metrics23.
Ghorbani et al. established an integrated Bayesian optimization and an
active learning approach for the accelerated design of Mg alloys24. Jain et al.
demonstrated that ensemble methods accurately predict the mechanical
properties of light weight Mg alloys with minimum error25. These studies
conﬁrm that the corrosion characteristics as a learnable variable with
composition and processing parameters. However, their limitations restrict
the accuracy of the model. The feature space constitutes the primary lim-
itations. Many of the Mg alloys ML models provide alloys as vectors that
include elemental weight fractions as compositions, processing conditions
and electrolyte properties. The model must independently identify ther-
modynamic and electrochemical relationships directly from data26. icorr is
associated with CR by Faraday’s law through equivalent weight and alloy
density, which can be calculated from composition but are rarely provided
as input features. The Pilling–Bedworth ratio which is responsible for
determining protective oxide layers is also unavailable. Miedema mixing
enthalpies, electronegativityvariations and atomicsize mismatch factors are

that physicochemical alloy descriptors are the most accurate predictors of

majority of Mg-based ML models28. The volume of data is considered as
secondarylimitation.AlmostallMLstudiesonMgalloycorrosionarebased

focussed on improving data-driven corrosion and alloy design. Machine
learning-assisted optimization of HEA coatings offer a similar approach for
generative augmentation in corrosion research31. Physics informed neural
networks have enhanced corrosion rate prediction by integrating physical
dependencies32. Data-driven microstructure property investigation on
magnesium alloys indicates crystal structure as primary predictor of cor-
rosion behaviour33. Chemically informed active learning and other data
driven approaches have demonstrated the ability to optimize materials with
few data entries34. The hybrid ﬁnite element and deep learning models have
been used to predict the corrosion of coated magnesium alloys35. These
results are the cutting edge of ML-based Mg-Li corrosion prediction.
However, ~85% variance threshold suggests that feature representation and
dataset size constitute limiting factors.
Generative adversarial network (GAN) offer a systematic approach to
the data volume constraint. Goodfellow et al. proposed the GAN metho-
dology trained to generate synthetic samples that are statistically indis-
tinguishable from the training distribution by employing a simultaneous
trained classiﬁer36. Two variations have acquired widespread use for tabular
material data, namely the WGAN-GP37, which improves training through
Lipschitz constrained model and NSGAN with feature match regularization
minimizing mode collapse through the use of feature space averaging38. In
the ﬁeld of corrosion, Woldesellasse and Tesfamariam increased the pre-
diction accuracy of neural network from 86% to 96% by applying condi-
tional GAN to augment pitting corrosion dataset39. Ma et al. additionally
implemented a conditional tabular GAN (CT-GAN) to predict the total
extentofpipingcorrosionandobtainedcomparableimprovements40.Liand
Birbilis have proposed the use of NSGAN and cardiGAN algorithms for
multiobjective MPEA optimization in the context of alloy design41–46.
Ghorbani et al. in their investigation demonstrated that NSGAN trained on
619 experimental MPEA corrosion dataset generatedsynthetic samples that
improved the random forest R2 for icorr from 0.38 to 0.83 and for Ecorr from
0.66 to 0.9047. The outcomes of these studies verify that GAN augmentation
is a signiﬁcant regularization method in corrosion environments with
limited data. In the current study, the ﬁrst use of GAN-based augmentation
for Mg-Li alloy corrosion prediction was demonstrated. It was also the ﬁrst
study to directly evaluate NSGAN with WGAN-GP on the same electro-
chemical dataset measured by distributional ﬁdelity and subsequent pre-
diction accuracy. In comparison with previous GAN studies on steels,
pitting data and MPEA, the current framework combines a physics
informed feature set of eight composition-derived thermodynamic and
electrochemical descriptors, a generalization oriented gap penalized Baye-
sian m-TPE objective and a physical constraint on synthetic data. The study
is the ﬁrst publicly available reproducible generative machine learning fra-
mework for Mg-Li corrosion.
The present investigation provides four major insights that address
signiﬁcant gaps in the literature: (1) a comprehensive dataset of 450 data on
Mg-Li corrosion was established from peer reviewed literature comprising a
complete range of composition, processing methods and electrolytic con-
ditions, (2) the dataset was modiﬁed based on the principle of Faraday’s law
by integrating thermodynamic descriptors such as E°mix, EW, ρalloy, PBR,
ΔHmix, Δχ, δ, VEC, (3) GAN architectures such as NSGAN with residual
generator blocks, spectral normalization, gradient clipping, cosine sche-
duling and feature matching loss, and WGAN-GP were trained and eval-

four corrosion target variables, namely corrosion potential (Ecorr, V vs SCE),
corrosion current density (icorr, µA/cm2), corrosion rate (CR, mm/yr) and
hydrogen evolution rate (mL/cm2/day). The crystal structure of the dataset
contains 481 entries of α + β phase microstructure with Li content varying
between 5.7 and 10.3 wt%, 382 entries of β phase microstructure with Li
content above 10.3 wt% and 137 entries of α phase microstructure with Li
content below 5.7 wt%. This distribution of Li content enables the model to
learn phase-dependent corrosion characteristics of Mg-Li alloys. Table 1

corrosion data and the ﬁrst direct comparison of NSGAN and WGAN-GP

Methods
The computational framework developed for predicting the corrosion
behaviour of Mg-Li alloys using generative adversarial network (GAN)
augmented machine learning is present in the methodology. The overall
architecture is illustrated in Fig. 28, which consists of ﬁve stages: (1) Data
acquisition and feature engineering from literature sourced electrochemical
measurements, (2) Data preprocessing and target transformation, (3) Dual
GAN training and synthetic data generation, (4) Machine learning models
and hyperparameter optimization through Optuna Bayesian m-TPE and
(5) Model interpretability, evaluation and statistical analysis.
Data acquisition and feature engineering
The dataset used in this work was systematically collected by extracting
electrochemical corrosion data from peer reviewed publications on Mg-Li
binary and multicomponent alloys from Elsevier, Springer, Wiley, MDPI,
ACS from 2003 to 202548–162. The initial dataset prepared contains N = 1000
entries from literatures considering alloy compositions and corrosion target
variables. The target variables in the literature were from deﬁned approach

Generative adversarial network (GAN)
Data availability remain to be a major challenge towards the corrosion
prediction of Mg-Li alloys. The total sample size (N = 450) remains limited
for ML predictions, especially when data are combined from numerous
publications and experimental investigations are not larger than 20 to 50
data points. GAN, initially proposed by Goodfellow et al.36, have recently
been modiﬁed for the augmentation of tabular data in material science and
corrosion engineering. GAN, learn the joint probability distribution of
actual data and generate new data points derived from it. This study
involved the trainingand comparison of two advantageous GAN variations,
namely a non-saturating GAN (NSGAN) utilizing stabilization approach
and a Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty (WGAN-GP).

In order to conﬁrm our ﬁndings, we utilized ﬁve ML models for each
target22,23. Random forest averages several regression trees, each generated
on a sample derived from the data and using a random subset of char-
acteristics at each split. Since the trees are independent of each other, the
model has minimal variance and can able to withstand noise in addition to
the correlated descriptors. XGBoost is a gradient boosting algorithm that
buildtreesprogressivelyﬁttingthenexttreetothecurrentensembleresidual
error. The complexity is managed by L1 and L2 regularization and column
subsampling. Support vector regression with radial basis function kernel
translatestheinputsintohigh-dimensionalspaceandﬁtsalinearfunctionin
the space, allowing smooth curves and dense sampling provided by aug-
mentation. AdaBoost creates shallow trees, with each subsequent tree
focussing on incorrectly categorized by earlier versions. CatBoost is a gra-
dient boosting technique that use ordered boosting and ordered target
statistics to eliminate the prediction bias associated with conventional
boosting. This enhances generalization on large tabular datasets while
simultaneously ﬁtting integer-coded categorical features. Consequently, the
ﬁve ML models comprise of bagging, three variants of boosting and a kernel
method.
The models examined each target using a six stage method. Stage 1:
Each model was trained with its standard conﬁgurations on the experi-
mental data and provide an unaugmented baseline. Stage 2 utilizes the same
experimental data on stage 1, but with gap penalized Bayesian HPO,
demonstrating the effect of tuning without synthetic data. Stage 3 and Stage
4 repeat the baseline and hyperparameters tuning on augmented dataset.
These stages are trained and evaluated purely on synthetic data, rather they
demonstrate the actual structure each generator captured with augmented
accuracy suggesting a more accurate generator. Stage 5 and 6 train and test
on combined experimental and augmented data. The analyses were carried
outforeachofNSGANand WGAN-GP.Ineachstage,thedatawassplit80/

the test set excluded from all ﬁtting, tuning and scaling to ensure that no test
