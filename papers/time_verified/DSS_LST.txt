ABSTRACT
The stability of duplex stainless steel (DSS) laser-surface textured (LST) in brine solutions is of significant interest for industrial
applications. The present study reports evidences that passive films on smooth DSS2205 surfaces (Blank) deteriorate early
(<96 h) if compared to oxidized and textured surfaces of LST samples (>96 h). This lower performance is detected in anodic polari-
zation and in accelerated cyclic polarization tests with increasing exposition time and in harsh conditions. Although both surfaces
maintain their chemical resistance against corrosion in noncyclic potentiodynamic experiments, the LST microstructure shows a
broader passivation area with a larger potential range than the control samples. Moreover, the DSS2205 microstructure patterned
surface is preserved. On the contrary, localized corrosion with macroscopic pits and microcracks is extended on the whole Blank
surface. The α/γ crystalline phase ratio was reduced in corroded flat surface, while it remains stable in the corroded LST specimens,
thanks to a significant formation of FeCr2O4 as a protective oxide film, as proved by Raman. Thus, LST treatments promise some
advantages over nontreated interfaces, particularly for metallic pieces that will be in contact or installed close to salt environments.
1 | Introduction
Duplex stainless steels (DSSs) are Fe–Cr–Ni alloys consisting of
ferrite and austenite phases with about 50%–50% ratio [1, 2]. Due
to the synergistic effect between these two phases, DSS alloys
exhibit higher toughness and better weldability than ferritic
stainless steels, as well as higher mechanical strength and better
corrosion resistance than austenitic ones [1, 2]. Their remarkable
engineering performance has led to an increasing number of
applications, mainly in corrosive environments such as paper-
making, food processing, marine and petroleum industries,
due to the combination of high mechanical strength, good tough-
ness, and high corrosion resistance. Concerning to their inter-
granular corrosion, the combination of the austenite–ferrite
boundary and the ferrite phase enables the precipitation of
chromium carbides without severely depleting chromium at
the phase boundaries [3, 4]. However, DSSs can suffer significant
corrosion when implemented in fields under extremely severe
service conditions [5–7]. In the last two decades, the main com-
mon causes of DSS failures in service were pitting corrosion
(27%), chloride-induced stress corrosion cracking (SCC) (27%),
sulfide stress cracking (18%), microbially induced corrosion
(9%), selective corrosion (9%), hydrogen-induced stress cracking
(5%), and crevice corrosion (5%) [5, 6, 8, 9].
The protection of DSSs against corrosive media is achieved due to
the passive film formed on the top surface, which is mainly com-
posed of mixed hydroxides and oxides [10]. In stable working
conditions, the degradation and rupture process of the passive
film is a continuous metal oxidation process. The film structure

and W are higher in the ferritic passive film than that in the aus-

film are higher than those in the ferritic one. Environment variables

the typical double-layer arrangement [23].
Regarding the effect of the environment on the corrosion protection
of 2205 duplex metals, hydrogen sulfide generates hydrogen ions via
the reacidification effect [20]. Adding hydrogen sulfide thins the
passive film, and consequently, the number of oxygen vacancies
increases as well and Fe2+ cations are consumed. Furthermore,
the influence of the applied potential on the passive film can be
divided into anodic and cathodic potentials, compared with the cor-
rosion potential (Ecorr). At low anodic potentials, the passive film is
composed of Cr2O3, FeO, NiO, and MoO2, which contains a high
density of cationic vacancies [17]. At high anodic potentials, the

The influence of SS alloying elements on pitting corrosion mech-
anisms can be summarized as: difficulty of surface stabilization
[24], passive barrier [25], formation of soluble precipitates [26],
soluble inclusions [27], insoluble inclusions [28], and wrapping
[6] mechanisms. As, for example, in the case of pitting corrosion
induced by precipitates, the corrosion may be initiated due to:
(i) Cr-depleted zones surrounding precipitates [29–32]; (ii) the
microgalvanic effect between Cr-enriched phase and Cr-depleted
phase [33–36]; (iii) the high-stress field around the precipitates
[34, 37], and (iv) the α 0-martensite presence induced by strain
[38]. Therefore, although DSSs are more resistant to SCC than
austenitic ones, they also suffer the chloride-induced SCC under
certain conditions, mainly when immersed in seawater and alka-
line environments [39, 40]. Then, the amount of pitting corrosion
initiated in the ferrite phase is significantly larger than that in the
austenite phase, according to Örnek et al. [41]. Moreover, the
cracking mechanism is associated with ferrite dissolution and
hydrogen embrittlement [42]. Although pitting corrosion is pref-
erentially initiated in the ferrite phase, the cracks mainly propa-
gate along the ferrite/ferrite boundaries and austenite/ferrite
phase boundaries. In hydrogen-induced stress cracking, phase
boundaries are strong hydrogen traps and hydrogen diffusion
pathways [43]. In sulfide stress cracking, this phenomenon is
mainly associated with the acidification effect of sulfide [44, 45].
Nowadays, different approaches have been used to modify the
surface properties of DSS components, achieving new chemical,
physical, morphological, and metallurgical surface properties
and enhancing the corrosion resistance. For instance, Raja et al.
[46] reported that DSS2205 treated at 400°C by plasma nitro-
carburizing exhibited the highest corrosion resistance, along
with a significant increase in surface hardness. Wang et al.
[47]. improved the erosion–corrosion resistance through a nitro-
carburizing treatment in a salt bath at 430°C. Majatan etal. [48]
observed an enhancement of the corrosion resistance of the elec-
trical discharge-treated 2205 DSS for biomedical applications.
Lohrengel et al. [49] applied the electrochemical machining in
NaNO3 with very good results. On the other hand, hot-working
processes such as additive manufacturing of complex shape parts
and welded joints of structures produce undesired structural and
microstructural changes that unavoidably deteriorate the corro-
sion resistance of the DSSs. To restore their corrosion resistance,
several postmanufacturing treatments of the welded interfaces
have been proposed, including annealing treatments [50, 51],
electron beam texturing [52], laser surface heat treatment of
welded interfaces [53], and combining the treatment of electron
beam cladding and subsequent nitrocarburizing [54].
More recently, laser-based texturing technique have been used
for the preparation of superhydrophobic surfaces in aqueous
solution, which are transformed in effective-protective passive
films [55]. Compared with other techniques, laser texturing
presents interesting advantages such as quick processing time,
simple operation, precise control of patterns, and compatibility
with multiple materials [56, 57]. Some studies have reported
the correlation between (super)hydrophobicity and the corrosion
resistance of laser-textured stainless steels such as AISI 304
[58–60], AISI 316 [61–65], AISI 420 [66], and 17-4 PH [67].
Recently, we have explored the corrosion resistance of DSS2205
[68] and 301LN [69–71] sheets in molten carbonate at 600°C,
after laser-surface texturing (LST). The laser texturing was car-
ried out with a pulsed laser machine, selecting adequate conditions,
to generate a periodic surface pattern. With this controlled surface
oxidation, the corrosion rate was reduced by 48%, if compared to
nontextured sheets. This substantial improvement has been attrib-
uted to the presence of oxide layers, composed of chromium oxide
(Cr2O3), nickel oxide (NiO), and magnetite phase (Fe3O4), which
were present at both the valleys and micropillars of the treated sur-
face. In the present work, the novelty is to evaluate the stability of
DSS 2205 (hereafter denoted DSS2205 for simplification) modified
by LST, after cyclic electrochemical tests, representing the worst-
case scenario for the metal protection. Therefore, a detailed analysis
was performed to investigate the effects of laser texturing on the
DSS2205 surface by means of electrochemical assays (potentiody-
namic polarization and cyclic potentiodynamic polarization) with
increasing time (life-span evaluation). The reversibility of corrosion
resistance due to the interaction of LST DSS2205 surfaces with
3.5 wt.% NaCl was not investigated before. Moreover, the surface
oxide wettability and microstructure analyses, before and after cor-
rosion affectation, were evaluated by water contact angle (WCA)
and scanning electron microscopy (SEM)/energy-dispersive spec-
troscopy (EDX) microscopy. The results were compared to smooth
DSS2205 surfaces (Blank), in the same conditions.

Surface Topography
The topography of the surfaces, with and without LST treatment,

2.4 | Electrochemical Assays: Corrosion
Evaluation
The potentiodynamic polarization experiments on LST and
Blank samples were conducted using an Autolab PGSTAT302N
Galvanostat equipped with the ECD module (Ecochimie, The
Netherlands). A three-electrode setup was employed in a one-
compartment cell containing 200 mL of naturally aerated 3.5%
NaCl solution, as the supporting electrolyte. The temperature
and pH were only monitored at the beginning and at the end
of 1-week experiments. The temperature did not vary from 23
to 25±1°C in the period of analysis and the pH decreased from
7.5 to 8.0, at beginning of test, to acidic values (6.0), after 1 week
(measured with pH test strips). DSS samples, both laser-treated
and untextured (Blank), served as the working electrodes
(WE) and the area of analysis was delimited by the cell hole
(0.1963 cm2), adapted with a rubber gasket for sealing. A plati-
num wire was used as the counter electrode (CE), and an Ag|
AgCl electrode containing KCl 3 M aqueous solution acted as
the reference electrode (RE) (E° = 0.197 V at 25°C vs. SHE).
Polarization analyses were carried out by measuring linear sweep
voltammetry (LSV) scans, using a negative potential starting at
−1.0 V and raising to positive values (vs. EOCP) and with a scan
rate of 1 mV s−1. The system was stabilized for 30 min before
recording the open-circuit potential (EOCP), based on previous
works reported [72, 73].
The same conditions were used to perform the cyclic polarization
(CP) analyses to evaluate the pitting corrosion resistances of both
sample types. In this case, two LSV scans were performed to cre-
ate a cyclic polarization. The first scan started at a potential of
−0.5 V and progressed to 1.5 V, while the second scan immedi-
ately followed, reversing from 1.5 V back to −0.5 V. The WE area
in this test was larger than in the potentiodynamic anodic polar-
ization assays (0.7854 cm2) in order to be able to evaluate pit and
crevices formations and the chemical modification of oxide layer
by SEM–EDX and Raman analyses in a broader zone. The scan
rate was set to 1mV s−1. As-prepared LST samples were used for
this analysis. Several measurements were conducted, with con-
trolled intervals, to monitor corrosion behavior over a 7-day
period. The electrochemical cell, the electrodes, and the electro-
lyte solution were the same as described above. Prior to electro-
chemical measurements, the samples were immersed in the
electrolyte for 30 min to obtain a steady state (EOCP), based on
the previous OCP stabilization time achieved with potentiody-
namic polarization experiments described above [72, 73].
2.5 | Surface Wettability
The static WCA measurements were conducted using a Krüss
Drop Shape Analyzer goniometer to evaluate the wettability of
the samples. The instrument was calibrated and utilized under
standard laboratory conditions at room temperature. For each
sample, ten measurements were taken to ensure accuracy and
repeatability. The water drop (volume of 0.5 μL) was deposited
in the delimited zones serving as working area for corrosion tests.
Variations on the WCA values were used to follow up the surface
wettability of the abraded samples before and after corrosion
assays. 3D topography maps of untreated and laser textured sur-
faces, prior and after the deposition of one droplet, were taken
with a confocal microscope Zeta-20, from KLA microscope.
3 | Results and Discussion
3.1 | Microstructure and Passivating Layer of
DSS2205 LST Samples
3.1.1
|
Microstructural and Topographical Analysis
After LST treatment, the surface composition and microstructure
of the samples were analyzed to assess the effects of LST in detail.
Figure 2 displays the SEM images of both the Blank and
LST-treated microstructures. The Blank sample displayed a
completely flat surface post-polishing (Figure 2a,b), while the
LST-treated sample exhibited characteristic groove patterns and
periodic wave formations induced by the laser (Figure 2c,f). The
images indicate that the grooves measured approximately 70 μm
in width between the valleys of the laser tracks, with each valley
measuring around 30 μm in width. As illustrated in Figure 2e,f,
the topography images of the LST sample reveal a microstructure
characterized by uniformly distributed, square-shaped micropil-
lars without pronounced grooves and valleys. Additionally, it is
crucial to highlight the consistent roughness observed across
both the valleys and the micropillars, which are adorned with
redeposited material from the laser track. This favorable result
is attributed to the carefully optimized laser parameters, includ-
ing scanning speed, pulse duration, and pulse fluence, all opti-
mized for the laser–metal interaction in the DSS2205 alloy.
Surface topography was further characterized using confocal
profilometry to confirm the efficacy of cleaning and texturing
processes. Figure 3 displays the 3D reconstructed images of
the Blank surface and the LST pattern. These images illustrate
that laser processing significantly increases surface roughness by
incorporating textural features. The width of the valleys was
approximately 30 μm, while the height of the valleys between
the micropillars ranged from 3 to 5 μm. The width dimensions
of the grooves and pillars obtained from the surface profile
are similar to those observed in the SEM images.

3.2 | Corrosion Resistance in Salt Solution and
Surface Characterization
3.2.1
|
Potentiodynamic Polarization Curves
Having proved that the DSS2205 LST samples were well pat-
terned and protected by passivating oxide layers, corrosion tests
were performed in concentrated salt solution to monitor the evo-
lution of such film stability. First, the flat surface of sheets with-
out laser texturing was subjected to polarization assays during
1 week. Figure 5a shows the potentiodynamic polarization curves
of Blank samples, before and after their immersion in 3.5 wt.% of
NaCl solution. There is no typical Tafel region during anodic
polarization due to the occurrence of transformation from acti-
vation to passivation. Therefore, in the anodic curve, a clear
Flade potential (Ef) at −0.43 V is observed in the first 6 h of
immersion time, in which the current density reverses and moves
to lower values (7.6×10−7A). The electrode surface was trans-
formed from active to passive state, with critical passivation cur-
rent density (icp) starting at 5.2×10−7A cm−2 (Table 2). Moreover,
the material presents a high range of concentration polarization
in the cathodic region, from −1.0 to −0.5 V. The formation of
well-known passive layers composed of Cr2O3, Fe2O3, Fe3O4,
Fe2(MoO4)3, and NiO above the SS surface helps to maintain
the corrosion current density (icorr) at low values (μA cm−2) with
increasing immersion time (Table 2, above 6 h) [77]. However,
the corrosion potentials (Ecorr) move to positive values and the
anodic curve slowly begins to lean toward the area of positive
current densities, with increasing potential (range from −0.3

In our previous studies [68], it has been proved that LST DSS2205
samples have thicker nonpassive and passive oxide layers composed
mainly of Fe3O4, Cr2O3, NiO, and amorphous or highly disordered
graphitic carbon (C), distributed by layers. When studying the LST
samples in contact with NaCl solutions, at the same conditions than
Blank (Figure 5a), a clear activation to positive current densities is
observed for the first case, i.e., in textured sample (Figure 5b). The
Ecorr is much more negative than in the Blank plates, as expected,
since the LST surface is already oxidized by the controlled laser
treatment. Additionally, this steady layer provokes the appearance
of a much larger passive range (from −0.60 to +0.07 V) than that
observed for the Blank sheets, without any apparent Flade poten-
tials. However, a pseudo-critical passivation current (ip-cp) was
determined when the current value goes back into the anodic zone
(Figure 5b), with progressive current depletion from 6 to 72 h of
polarization test. Moreover, after 3 days of contact with the brine
solution, the LST surface remains highly stable until 1 week, with
no variation either in the Ecorr or in the icorr data (Table 2). Similarly,
the icorr was obtained by extrapolation analysis due to the absence of
Tafel behavior. The values are expressed in Table 2 for comparison
with the Blank samples.
As explained in previous sections, the laser energy applied
(11.81 J cm−2) and the scanning speed (3.6 mm s−1) chosen do
not exceed the heating limits that could cause an excessive melt-
ing of the passive layer of DSS2205, as observed in other studies
[78]. Thus, the compact structure of protective oxides is the
responsible for the large passivated zone found in those samples.
Detailed inspection of SEM images also proved that the textured
and rough topography obtained with the nanosecond-pulsed
laser treatment is mostly preserved after 7 days of corrosion anal-
ysis, corroborating with the high stability of this oxide layer
(Section 3.2.2).

phenomenon was observed only in a few zones of analysis. Deeper

gen in austenite. Combining SEM and EBSD, authors showed
that some small pits appear on the γ-phase and become SCC
nucleation sites.

3.3 | Influence of Surface Wettability in the
Corrosion Protection
It is widely known that the wettability of a metal surface is con-
nected with its micromorphology. In laser-induced surface tex-
tures it depends on the widths of microgrooves [57]. In the
present study, the patterning model (microgrooves width and
height) was preserved by the fixed conditions detailed in
Section 2.2. The WCA measurements showed that the laser-
treated samples exhibited significantly higher hydrophobicity
compared to the Blank samples (Figure 12a). The LST substrate
before polarization tests is superhydrophobic, with WCA values
higher than 150° [65]. This enhanced hydrophobicity has been
extensively investigated and it is attributed to the microscale sur-
face roughness induced by the laser treatment [60, 86]. The laser
etching process creates a grooved pattern that alters the surface
energy [65, 87]. Surface roughness can amplify the intrinsic
hydrophobic properties by trapping air pockets and reducing
the actual contact area between the water droplets and the sur-
face. Consequently, water droplets are more likely to bead up
rather than spread out on the microtextured surface, resulting in
higher contact angles (i.e., an increased hydrophobicity). Thus,
representing an appropriate way to delay any entrance of the
aggressive electrolyte to the base steel [61, 80].
For corrosion protection, it is relevant to evaluate the evolution of
the WCA after a prolonged exposition to the aqueous solution. As
shown in Figure 12a, DSS2205 bare surfaces are hydrophobic,
with WCA values lower than those of LST surfaces, as expected
given its smoother surface. Moreover, WCA is almost indifferent
with respect to samples analyzed before (105.3±1.5°) and after
(97.3±8.2°) potentiodynamic tests (noncyclic). In the case of LST
surfaces, the superhydrophobicity (WCA 153.9±2.3°) decreases

4 | Conclusions
The stability of DSSl 2205 modified by LST in brine solutions, after
cyclic electrochemical tests, has been satisfactorily evaluated.
Although flat and nontextured surfaces of DSS2205 are considered
one of the highest resistant steels in marine environments, ferrite
seems to be dissolved in prolonged electrochemical cyclic experi-
ments, causing the appearance of micropits, as proved in the pres-
ent study. By the contrary, the LST alloy maintains its patterning
of grooves and pillars designed by the nanosecond-laser treatment,
even after the severe damage caused by 1 week of brine solution
immersion and cyclic potentiodynamic testing, preserving its high
hydrophobicity
properties
and
avoiding
crevice
corrosion.
Therefore, the present study demonstrated that laser-texturing
treatments, with controlled laser fluence and scanning speed, gen-
erate a stable oxide passive film on the DSS2205 top face, which
enhances the stability of stainless steel matrix in brine solution
(3.5 wt.%) and under harsh conditions.
Evaluation of the surface topography and corrosion products by
FTIR, Raman, SEM–EDX and XRD analyses corroborated the
abovementioned results. The samples without laser texturing
treatment had evident signs of crevice localized corrosion,
whereas LST samples maintained the patterned grooves intact.
This protective effect is attributed to the obtaining of FeCr2O4
oxides in the passive film localized at the grooves, which was
induced by the LST process. Therefore, a continuous overlap
of the laser tracks in LST treatment is necessary to achieve such
good performance.
One of the most important conclusions is that flat and SLT
DDS2205 stainless steel cannot be recommended in systems
which will involve immersion of the pieces in NaCl solutions
with cyclic electrochemical procedures, such as in electrolysis
pumps for generating chlorine gas in swimming pools, in osmosis
desalination plants, among other sectors. However, SLT DDS2205
can be employed in non-submerged zones with chlorine atmo-
sphere (ports, ships, shipyards, coastal areas) under continuous
monitoring (maintenance) because the passive oxide layers are
more stable than untextured ones.
