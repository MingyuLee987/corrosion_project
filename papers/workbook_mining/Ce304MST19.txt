Keywords: Ce treatment; 304 stainless steel; MnS inclusion; Corrosion resistance 
Abstract: The effect of inclusion modification on pitting corrosion of 304 stainless 
steel with different cerium contents was studied by accelerated immersion test, and 
electrochemical measurement. The results show that in the absence of cerium, MnS and 
composite oxide are the dominating inclusions in stainless steel, and the average size 
reaches 8.6 μm, while the self-corrosion potential of steel is only -348.52 mV. By 
adding the cerium, the inclusions were gradually modified into spherical inclusions 
containing cerium, the average size is reduced, and the corrosion resistance of the steel 
is improved. When the content of cerium reaches 0.012 %, the MnS inclusions are all 
modified into spherical inclusions containing cerium, and the self-corrosion potential 
of steel reaches -311.25 mV, which has the best corrosion resistance. When the content 
of cerium increases above 0.012 %, the shape of the inclusions becomes irregular, and 
the size increases, resulting in a decrease in corrosion resistance. 
1 Introduction 
Stainless steel is widely used in chemical, petrochemical and medical industries 
due to its good corrosion resistance. However, stainless steel is prone to pitting 
corrosion in the chlorine-containing environments. Pitting corrosion is among the most 
dangerous modes of damage to stainless steel, not only for the difficulty in early 
detection as localized penetration, but also because it often serves as the precursor to 
crack initiation in stress corrosion and corrosion fatigue [1].  
Manganese sulphide (MnS) inclusions in steel are known to be often precursor 
sites for pitting corrosion, which directly affects the initiation and expansion of pitting 
corrosion [2]. To effectively remove and control the MnS inclusions generated in the 
process of steelmaking and continuous casting, inclusion removal technologies such as 
gas stirring [3], electromagnetic purification [4], slag washing [5], and filter [6] have 
been developed. In addition, the effect of MnS inclusion on the properties of steel can 
be reduced by adding modifier to the liquid steel. At present, the main inclusions 
modification technologies include Ca treatment, Mg treatment, calcium-magnesium 
treatment, and rare earth treatment. Zhang et al. [7] indicated that Ca treatment can 
modify the MnS inclusions. However, if Ca reacts with sulfur to form CaS with a higher 
melting point than MnS, it is easy to cause nozzle clogging; if Ca reacts with oxygen 
to form CaO, the sulfide in steel is not completely modified, which deteriorates the

Cerium (Ce) has a strong affinity towards O and S, and react with these elements 
to form spherical or ellipsoid Ce-containing inclusions, which can significantly modify 
the sulfide inclusions in steel [12]. In addition, the compound formed by the reaction 
of Ce with S and O is easily removed in the molten steel [13]. Therefore, the Ce-
containing steel can greatly reduce the corrosion tendency of the matrix induced by the 
MnS inclusion. In this paper, different amounts of Ce were added into stainless steel 
through laboratory high temperature experiments to obtain Ce-containing stainless steel. 
And the effects of Ce content on corrosion resistance of stainless steel were studied by 
means of scanning analysis, accelerated immersion test, and electrochemical 
experiments. 
2 Experimental Design 
2.1 Material Preparation 
The commercial 304 stainless steel was provided by the Taiyuan Iron & Steel Co., 
Ltd (Shanxi Provence, China), and its chemical composition is listed in Table I. To 
obtain cerium-containing stainless steel, about 800 g of commercial 304 stainless steel 
were melted in a high-temperature electric pipe with a MoSi2 heating element (Tianjin 
Taisite Instrument Co., Ltd, China). The furnace atmosphere was kept low in oxygen 
content on a constant inflow of purified argon gas, 400 L·h-1, from the bottom of the 
furnace. After the molten steel temperature reached 1873 K (1600oC) and kept for 30 
min at this temperature, a certain amount of cerium warped in pure iron foil were added 
to the molten steel. A molybdenum rod was used to stir the molten steel. After the 
stirring, the molten steel was maintained for 30 min, and then the solidification samples 
could be obtained by water quenching. The quenching process was performed in water 
at room temperature, i.e., 293 K (20oC), and the cooling rate was about 45 oC·s-1. During 
this study, four different mass fractions of cerium, 0, 0.006, 0.012, and 0.018 wt.%, 
were evaluated during the laboratory experiments.

2.2 Experimental Methods 
2.2.1 Inclusion Detection 
In order to clarify the inclusions in steel samples, 10×10×10 mm3 cubic samples 
were cut from sample steel, then ground with 2000 grit silicon carbide paper and 
polished by 0.5 μm diamond paste. After polishing, about 100 typical inclusions

selected randomly from each steel sample were examined with scanning electron 
microscope (SEM) and energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS) (model ProX) to 
determine the morphology, size and type of inclusion. 
2.2.2 Electrochemical Measurements  
To reveal the pitting tendency of test steels with different Ce content, 10×10×5 
mm3 cubic samples were cut from sample steel, and the exposed area (test surface) was 
10×10 mm2. A copper wire was attached to the rear side of each specimen and mounted 
in an epoxy resin, and the test surface was ground with 2000 grit silicon carbide paper 
and polished by 0.5 μm diamond paste. After polishing, the potentiodynamic 
polarization tests were conducted with VersaSTATMC electrochemical workstation in 
a three-electrode cell system, where the specimen was used as the working electrode, a 
platinum plate was used as the counter electrode, and a saturated calomel electrode 
(SCE) was used as the reference electrode. The potentiodynamic polarization 
measurements were measured at a scanning rate of 0.5 mV/s in 3.5wt.% NaCl solution 
at 298 K (25 oC), and the scanning range was ± 0.5 V (SCE) relative to the open circuit 
potential.  
2.2.3 Immersion Corrosion Tests 
To clarify the correlation between inclusions in test steels with different Ce 
contents and pitting corrosion, test specimens were immersed in the corrosion solution 
for which comprised 350 ml deionized water with 69.9 g FeCl3·6H2O and 20 ml HCl 
(36~38 wt.%). Each specimen was sealed with epoxy resin except for the test surface 
to avoid formation of porosity and cracks around the surface. The immersion time is 0 
s, 30 s and 90 s, respectively. After reaching the set immersion time, the test specimen 
was immediately taken out of the corrosion solution, rinsed, and dried. The corrosion 
morphology of immersion specimen was observed with SEM. 
3 Results and Discussion 
3.1 Effect of Ce Content on the Morphology and Type of Inclusions 
Figure 1 shows the morphology and type of inclusions in test steels with different 
cerium contents. Without adding the cerium, the strip-like MnS inclusion and irregular 
shaped Al2O3-SiO2-MnO composite inclusion are mainly formed in the 304 stainless 
steel, which is presented in Figure 1(a), and the size of the inclusions reaches more than 
10 μm. After adding 0.006 wt.% of Ce, the inclusions in test steel were modified to 
form Ce-containing oxygen-sulfur composite inclusions (CexOySz) and insufficiently 
modified MnS-CexOySz composite inclusions. The shape of the inclusions was 
subsphaeroidal, and the size is reduced to about 7 to 10 μm, as shown in Figure 1(b). 
Figure 1(c) shows the morphology of inclusions in test steel with addition of 0.012 wt.% 
of Ce, the inclusions are sufficiently modified to form a single distribution of Ce-
containing oxide inclusions (CexOy), Ce-containing sulfide inclusions (CexSy), and 
CexOySz inclusions. The shape of the inclusions is spherical, and the size is between 3 
and 10 μm. There is no MnS inclusion in test steel, indicating that the addition of 0.012 
wt.% of Ce can completely modify the strip-like MnS inclusions and irregular shaped 
Al2O3-SiO2-MnO composite inclusions. After adding 0.018 wt.% of Ce, there are 
mainly CexSy and CexOySz inclusions in test steel. The shape of the inclusions becomes 
irregular, and the size reaches more than 10 μm.

To further explain the change of the properties of inclusions in steel, 64 fields of 
view were randomly selected on the surface of the specimen by SEM, and the EDS of 
the inclusions in the field of view was analyzed. The electron microscope has a 
magnification of 1000 times and a field of view of 4.7 mm2. The size of inclusions was 
statistically analyzed by Image-Pro Plus. Figures 2 and 3 show the proportion of various

test steel without addition of Ce, there are mainly MnS inclusions, the proportion of 
MnS inclusion reaches 64 %, and there are also some irregularly shaped Al2O3-SiO2-
MnO composite inclusions. The average size of inclusions reaches 8.6 μm. After adding 
0.006 wt.% of Ce, because the MnS inclusions in test steel are not sufficiently modified, 
the MnS-CexOySz composite inclusions still exist in test steel. In addition, most of the 
inclusions in test steel are modified into Ce-containing inclusions, and the proportion 
of CexOy, CexSy and CexOySz reaches 70 %. The average size of inclusions is reduced

3.2 Effect of Ce Content on Corrosion Resistance of Test Steel 
Figure 4 shows the morphology of inclusions in test steel with different Ce 
contents after immersion for 0 s, 30 s, and 120 s. As shown in Figure 4(a), when the 
test steel without addition of Ce, micro-crevices formed in the inclusion/matrix 
boundary at 30 s. After immersion corrosion for 2 min, the crevices in the 
inclusion/matrix boundary increased further, and serious pitting corrosion occurred on 
the surface of the steel. After adding 0.006 wt.% of Ce, slight corrosion phenomenon 
occurred after the test steel was immersed for 2 min, as shown in Figure 4(b). When the 
Ce content in test steel reaches 0.012 wt.%, there is almost no change in the surface of 
the steel after immersion corrosion, as shown in Figure 4(c), indicating that the test steel 
has excellent corrosion resistance. After adding 0.018 wt.% of Ce, pitting corrosion

MnS inclusions are mainly present in stainless steel without addition of Ce, and 
its morphology, size and distribution seriously affect the properties of steel [14]. In the 
corrosion process, the passivation film in the inclusion/matrix boundary is weak, which 
is easily damaged under the corrosion of corrosive Cl-, resulting in the activation of the 
matrix around the inclusions. Corrosion of the matrix produces a large amount of Fe3+ 
and Cr3+, as shown in Eqs. (1) and (2). The hydrolysis reaction of Fe3+ and Cr3+ 
produces H+, as shown in Eqs. (3) and (4), lead to local acidification in the 
inclusion/matrix boundary. The MnS inclusions are corroded to form HS-, as shown in 
Eq. (5). H+ and HS- further corrode the matrix around the inclusions, which deteriorates 
the corrosion resistance of the stainless steel.

When the test steel with addition of 0.012 wt.% of Ce, the MnS inclusions were 
completely modified into spherical Ce-containing inclusions, which reduced the 
possibility of matrix corrosion induced by MnS inclusions. In addition, the shape of the 
Ce-containing inclusions is spherical, which has better fusion with matrix. The 
passivation film in the inclusion/matrix boundary is not easily damaged by the 
aggressive ions, which greatly reduces the probability of inclusion inducing matrix 
corrosion. When the stainless steel adding 0.006 wt.% of Ce, the MnS inclusions are 
not completely modified, and there are still existed separate MnS inclusions, the 
presence of MnS inclusions induce corrosion of the matrix. Therefore, the corrosion 
resistance is lower than that of the stainless steel containing 0.012 wt.% of Ce. After 
adding 0.018 wt.% of Ce, although there is no MnS inclusion in steel, the shape of the 
Ce-containing inclusions is irregular. The passivation film in the inclusion/matrix 
boundary is easily damaged by the aggressive ions, causing the matrix to be corroded. 
4 Conclusions 
(1) Ce can modify the MnS inclusions in steel to form Ce-containing oxide, Ce-
containing sulfide and Ce-containing oxygen-sulfur composite inclusions.

(2) In corrosive environments, the corrosion products produced by the dissolution 
of MnS inclusion aggravate the corrosion of steel. When Ce is added, the Ce-containing 
inclusions greatly reduces the probability of inclusion inducing corrosion of matrix.  
(3) There is an optimal addition amount of Ce in steel. under the optimal addition 
amount, the MnS inclusions are completely modified to form spherical Ce-containing 
inclusions, and inclusions have the least tendency to induce corrosion of the matrix.
